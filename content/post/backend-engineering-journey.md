For the next six months, I’m making **backend engineering** my main technical learning focus.

I don’t want this to become another folder of bookmarks, unfinished courses, or notes I never look at again. My plan is simple: **learn something, use it, then write about what actually happened**.

That means this series will include the parts that usually disappear from polished tutorials — the concepts that took time to click, the assumptions I got wrong, bugs I had to trace, small experiments that changed my understanding, and the projects where everything finally connected.

## What I’ll be learning

The direction will move from backend fundamentals toward production-minded engineering. I expect the journey to include topics such as:

- HTTP, APIs, and server-side fundamentals
- backend frameworks and application structure
- relational databases and data modelling
- authentication, authorization, and security
- testing and debugging
- caching and performance
- containers and deployment
- cloud and DevOps fundamentals
- system design and scaling decisions

The exact order may change as I build more, because I want the learning path to respond to real problems instead of following a checklist blindly.

## How I’ll write about it

I’m aiming to document something regularly, but I’m not going to force every entry into a long tutorial.

Some posts may be a detailed breakdown of a concept. Others may be a short note about one bug, one experiment, or one thing I finally understood that day.

What matters is that each entry reflects **something I actually learned or tested**.

## Why I’m doing this publicly

Writing forces me to notice gaps in my own understanding. If I can’t explain why something works, I probably don’t understand it well enough yet.

It also gives this portfolio a better record of progress. Instead of only showing finished projects, I want it to show the engineering thinking that develops between them.

This page is the starting point. The posts that follow will live under **Learning → Backend Engineering Journey**.
